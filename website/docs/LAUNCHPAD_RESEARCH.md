# Launchpad Research — October 5, 2026

## What we can support

Reviewed current first-party sites in a browser, project documentation, and indexed X references. Screenshots of Bags, BONKfun, Pump, and the paused apps.fun deployment are under `Evidence/Creative Refresh/Research`. This is a product and design comparison, **not a verified October 5 X trend ranking**. Search-indexed social summaries and earlier posts are discovery leads. They do not prove current popularity, relative volume, or why a platform gained attention. The explanations below are design hypotheses.

## Observations and adaptations

| Reference | Observed attribute | Why it may spread (inference) | NUCALORIC adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pump | Direct creation, discovery, social activity, visible fee documentation | A short path to action and a constantly changing feed provide reasons to revisit | Direct project kits, persistent drafts, and a clear next step into Studio or the launch builder |
| Bags | Creator-centered home, fee-sharing story, creator profiles, programmable tools | People can describe the creator benefit in one sentence and connect it to identifiable people | Project purpose, builder/community allocation planning, and inspectable project links |
| BONKfun | Community framing, hot-project discovery, watchlist and creation/progress views | A recognizable community and understandable progress give users something to discuss | Discover example projects by apps, agents, tools, or community; show a first milestone in the brief |
| Raydium LaunchLab | Documented fee beneficiaries, graduation behavior, and distinct creator/LP rights | Clarity makes a complex process easier to explain and evaluate | Evidence is explicitly self-reported, proposed splits are planning only, and no frontend control claims financial execution |
| apps.fun (indexed description) | App-oriented launches, ownership proof, and developer utility | A functioning app is easier to explain than a ticker alone | Repository/demo evidence fields, a useful-project narrative, and exportable briefs |
| PAID / Ignition (historical) | Applicant screening and a focused launch workflow in a 2021 first-party article | Selection and a clear application journey can reduce confusion | Purpose-first kits and a reviewable project brief; no invented vetting badge |

### Pump

The current fee page separates creator, protocol, and LP fees and discusses transaction-specific behavior. Its public home exposes discovery and social updates. The transferable pattern is a clear action and understandable context, rather than copying speculative profit displays. NUCALORIC keeps its existing launch builder and adds a Studio route before it. [Official fee documentation](https://pump.fun/docs/fees), [site](https://pump.fun/).

### Bags

The current homepage emphasizes creators, token discovery, and earnings. Its help center describes creator royalties and launch-time splits. The official Bags Play repository describes reusable, cloud-run token fee automations. This supports exploring developer tools as a distinct product layer. NUCALORIC adds an editable purpose, capability shortlist, and a proposed allocation; it does not implement Bags fees or advertise a matching rate. [Help center](https://support.bags.fm/en/articles/13434876-royalty-earned), [Bags Play](https://github.com/bagsfm/play), [site](https://bags.fm/).

### BONKfun

The inspected public interface groups discovery around hot projects, creation timing, progress, and watchlists, with a community-centered headline. NUCALORIC’s adaptation is an example project lens that helps visitors find what someone is making. No BONK token statistics or ranking claims are copied. [Official site](https://www.bonk.fun/).

### Raydium LaunchLab

Current first-party documentation distinguishes bonding-curve creator fees, CPMM creator fees, and platform-owned locked LP rights following the August 17, 2026 migration upgrade. A frontend must not conflate these. The lesson for NUCALORIC is to make ownership and financial effects legible. The Studio allocation is a draft for future project income, never a transaction or promised return. [Official creator-fee documentation](https://github.com/raydium-io/raydium-docs-v1/blob/main/products/launchlab/creator-fees.mdx).

### apps.fun and PAID: limits

apps.fun’s indexed how-it-works page describes app ownership and utility, but the live deployment was paused during this inspection. Treat those descriptions as historical/indexed context until the live product returns. The PAID reference is a historical Ignition UI article, not proof of a currently trending Solana-native launchpad. The founder’s exact PAID reference remains unconfirmed. [Indexed apps.fun page](https://www.apps.fun/how-it-works), [PAID’s historical UI article](https://paidnetwork.medium.com/ignition-launchpad-ui-sneak-peek-e00ae36c5fb6).

An indexed X topic about Bags describes a creator-fee narrative, but the direct topic URL returned access restrictions. Its January topic date is not evidence of an October trend. An older public X profile includes a discussion of using creator fees for community funding; that is an example of a story people can retell, not causal proof of product growth. [Indexed Bags topic](https://x.com/i/trending/2012275707494179269), [public discussion](https://x.com/JbTheQuant).

For a future trend study: capture a dated sample of original official posts, unique authors, reach, project launches, and repeat usage. Compare several days, avoid paid-promotion counts, and distinguish product adoption from price attention. This task adds the useful product patterns without fabricating social proof.

## Implemented now

- Three project kits on Home and Registry.
- Registry shortlist that persists on this browser.
- Studio: project purpose, first milestone, workspace type, self-reported repository/demo links, income-allocation planning, save and Markdown export.
- Explore: app, agent, tool, and community filters over explicitly labeled example project concepts.
- Account: shortcut to the saved project brief.

Real deployments, verified ownership, public project profiles, payouts, and on-chain fee routing still need authenticated backend integrations. The existing Paymenter terminal gate remains in place.
